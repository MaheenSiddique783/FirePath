# User Analysis

Who we're building FirePath for, and what we think they need. Everything here comes from our own research into who lives with wildfire risk in Canada. 

### Who

- Our focus is people who aren't fire professionals but live with wildfire risk anyway
    - Cabin and cottage owners near forests
    - Farmers and ranchers
    - Small towns, rural municipalities and volunteer fire departments
    - Campers and hikers
    - Travellers heading somewhere fire-prone
- We are not building for fire crews. They already have FBP Go, REDapp and professional software
- We are also not an evacuation tool, so the app always points people to official alerts

### Cabin and cottage owners

- Who they are
    - Families and retirees who live in the city and drive out to the cabin on weekends and through the summer
- What they do
    - They check the weather app before heading out
    - At the cabin they're on their phone, not a laptop
- What they need
    - To know how a fire near the cabin would behave today, and a heads-up when the danger climbs
    - Fuel codes and index numbers won't mean much to them, so it has to be in plain words

### Farmers and ranchers

- Who they are
    - People working farmland or pasture in rural Saskatchewan, Alberta or Manitoba
- What they do
    - They're outside most of the day and check wind and the forecast on their phone between jobs
    - Spring and fall is when they burn stubble or brush, and it's also when grass fires are most likely
- What they need
    - A quick answer to "is it safe to burn today?" and "if a grass fire started, how fast would it run with this wind?"
    - It has to take seconds, without typing in a pile of numbers

### Small towns, RMs and volunteer fire departments

- Who they are
    - Municipal staff, councillors and volunteer firefighters
    - Everyone wears several hats and nobody on staff is a fire behaviour specialist
- What they do
    - They get fire information from provincial bulletins, emails and the CWFIS website
    - They pass updates on to residents, often through Facebook and the town website
- What they need
    - A quick way to see the danger around their area and explain it to residents without learning expert software
    - Being able to look at a few spots at once, like the townsite and the campground, would help

### Campers and hikers

- Who they are
    - Mostly people comfortable with apps and maps, like students, young adults and families
- What they do
    - They plan trips on their phone with tools like Google Maps and AllTrails
    - Most of their trips happen on long weekends and through the summer
- What they need
    - Answers to "can we have a campfire tonight?" and "is there a fire nearby that could reach us?"
    - Cell signal is often weak where they go, so the danger level has to load fast

### Travellers heading to fire-prone areas

- Who they are
    - Tourists and families driving through places like the Rockies in summer, sometimes from out of province
- What they do
    - They plan ahead, compare options online and check the weather a few days before leaving
    - Most don't know the area well
- What they need
    - To check fire danger for each stop, both close to the trip and further out
    - Some may also care whether a place has burned before
